# Astrological Knowledge for Chart-Specific Question Construction

How a planet's dignity, house placement, and lordship combine to predict what its dasha period brings and how it delivers results through a specific house.

---

## Combining Planet + Dignity + House + Lordship

A planet's effect during its dasha or through its Bhava Chalit house is the product of four layers:

**Layer 1 — Core Nature**: What the planet inherently signifies (Saturn = discipline/delay/karma, Mars = energy/ambition/conflict, etc.). Reference: `dasha-signatures.md` for each planet's core themes.

**Layer 2 — Dignity Modifier**: How the planet's sign placement affects its expression.
- Exalted → themes expressed powerfully, with ease, at their highest potential
- Own Sign → themes expressed naturally, comfortably, reliably
- Friend's sign → somewhat comfortable, themes expressed through the friend planet's coloring
- Neutral/Enemy sign → themes are present but less clean, mixed with friction
- Debilitated → themes expressed through struggle, frustration, inversion
- Debilitated with Neecha Bhanga → struggle that transforms into unconventional strength; the debilitation doesn't disappear but gets overridden by a stronger force

**Layer 3 — House Modifier**: Which life arena the planet's results manifest through. The Bhava Chalit house (not the Rashi house) determines where results are DELIVERED. Reference: `house-themes.md` for what each house signifies in practical terms.

**Layer 4 — Lordship Modifier**: Which houses the planet rules (based on the ascendant sign). A planet activates the matters of the houses it rules. If Mercury rules the 4th house (home, mother, education), Mercury's dasha will involve those themes regardless of where Mercury sits.

To determine what a planet's dasha period should feel like in this specific chart, or what "this planet delivering through House X" looks like in practice: combine all four layers. The result is a specific, non-generic prediction that can be tested against lived experience.

## What Makes a Question Chart-Specific

A generic question: "Was the period from 2015-2022 disruptive?"
A chart-specific question: one where the options are derived from THIS chart's specific planetary data.

The difference matters because chart-specific questions test the chart. Generic questions get generic confirmations that don't distinguish one birth time from another.

For a question to be chart-specific, the options must be generated from the four-layer combination above. The options should describe concrete life patterns that this specific planet-in-this-dignity-in-this-house would produce — and include at least one option that describes what a DIFFERENT placement would produce. This creates a genuine test rather than a confirmation exercise.

## Everyday Language Translation

Astrological concepts translate to everyday life patterns:

| Astrological Concept | Everyday Language |
|:---|:---|
| Planet in 12th house | "operates behind the scenes," "happens in isolation," "connected to foreign/distant contexts" |
| Planet in 11th house | "manifests through social connections," "income/gains-oriented," "visible success through networks" |
| Planet in 10th house | "defines career identity," "shapes public reputation," "institutional/authority-based" |
| Debilitated planet | "feels frustrating," "the thing you know you should be able to do but can't consistently access" |
| Exalted planet | "comes naturally," "operates at full power," "the area of life where things work out" |
| Neecha Bhanga | "struggled at first but became a strength," "unconventional ability that outperforms conventional approaches" |
| Planet ruling 8th house active in dasha | "transformation, crisis, hidden things surfacing, dealing with loss or inheritance" |
| Planet ruling 7th house active in dasha | "partnerships central, marriage-related events, business through others" |

The person being questioned doesn't need to know astrology. The question options should describe life patterns they can recognize from their own experience.

## Boundary Planet Question Design

When a planet sits near a Bhava Chalit cusp, the question is: which house does it deliver through?

The key to a good boundary question: the two house options must be clearly distinguishable in lived experience. Some house pairs are easy to distinguish (11th vs 12th: social vs isolated). Others are harder (4th vs 5th: home vs creativity — both are personal, positive arenas).

For harder-to-distinguish pairs, make the question planet-specific. "Does your DISCIPLINE (Saturn) operate through..." is sharper than "does your life in this area tend toward..." because the planet's specific nature creates the contrast between the two houses.

Combine the planet's core nature with each candidate house's themes from `house-themes.md` to produce two distinct life-pattern descriptions the person can choose between.
