## My read

You’ve described Oliver as the stable, private part of your life, and your family as people who know you well. You’ve also said work takes everything, and that you haven’t fully made space for life outside the lab. So I don’t think the main question is whether you care. It’s whether the people you care about can feel that in the shape of your week.

## Your family

Your chart puts strong weight on home, family and security, and that broadly fits your account: your mother knows you deeply, and your brothers can push back in a way others may not. The real-life pressure is specific: you’re worried about time with your mom, your dad’s death still feels ungrieved, and the distance from Annie weighs on the family. I wouldn’t turn any of that into a prediction that something bad is about to happen. I would take it as a reason not to wait for a crisis before making contact a regular part of life.

For the next few years, I’d aim for **reliable presence rather than one grand family gesture**: set a cadence for seeing or calling your mom and siblings; make some of that time genuinely unhurried; and let your relationship with Annie be what it is for now without making yourself responsible for resolving it. You can support your family without becoming the fixer. And your grief for your dad may need its own space, separate from work and from the family’s current strain.

One chart caveat: your birth time is approximate, and the relationship-focused divisional chart changes with a very small time shift. So I’m keeping this to broad themes and timing—not claiming to know the fate of your partnership or to date a family event.

The practical focus I’d choose is simple: **put time with Oliver and your family on the calendar before work fills the space.** I’m asking whether this concern is coming from Oliver or mainly from you because the next step is different if he has asked for more than if you’re anticipating that you may be neglecting something he hasn’t said is missing.
