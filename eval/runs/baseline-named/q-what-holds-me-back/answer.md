## What tends to hold you back

**1. You take on too many important futures.**  
You know the principle: let smaller opportunities go to focus on step changes. But you’ve also said that in practice you spread yourself too thin—across boards, companies, and bets. My guess is that this isn’t just poor scheduling. You’re genuinely interested in many large possibilities, and each one can feel too important to turn down. The cost is that attention gets divided, and the life you mean to make room for keeps being postponed.

**2. You often turn emotional weight into work.**  
You’ve said you handled your father’s death by working, and aren’t sure you properly grieved. You’ve wondered whether you looked back at the end of Loopt and your relationship with Nick. Your mother’s observation—that you keep a lot inside and it comes out as stress in your body—fits with that. I don’t read this as a lack of feeling. More likely, you’re very good at staying functional while something painful remains unprocessed. That can help you through a crisis, but it can also leave work occupying the space where grief, rest, or closeness might go.

**3. Your directness and drive to take charge can outrun the relational moment.**  
You’ve described a lifelong instinct to pick what you believe and say it plainly, even if people are upset; your brother’s childhood summary was that you had to win and be in charge. Those traits can make you effective when a group needs clarity and someone willing to act. They can also make it harder to notice when people need listening, patience, or room to disagree—not just a decision. You already seem alert to that risk, which is a real strength.

If I had to condense it: **you’re not short on ambition or self-awareness; you’re more likely to get stuck by keeping too many high-stakes possibilities alive and carrying private feelings through work instead of giving them their own space.**
